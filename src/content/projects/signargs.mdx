SignArgs for Unity lets your application show a PDF, take a handwritten signature and finalize the signed document where the signer stands: the signature stamped in place, an audit page appended, the file hashed, and a set of records written that anyone can check later. It needs no server, no account for the signer and no network, so it works on a site, in a van or in a basement.

## How it works

- **The signature is bound to what was shown.** Every signature record ties together the exact bytes of the document, a hash of every page as it was drawn on screen, every input event of the stroke with the device's timestamp, and the sentence the signer agreed to.
- **Confirm, then finalize.** Confirming records one signature and grows the audit chain. Finalizing then does everything that changes the document once, for all signatures together.
- **A bundle anyone can verify.** Each finished session leaves the signed PDF, the original, the signature records, the strokes, a hash-linked audit chain and a manifest of SHA-256 hashes. Checking one needs a SHA-256 tool, not SignArgs.
- **Evidence, honestly labelled.** What can be proven is recorded as fact; what cannot, such as the device's clock or what your application reports, is labelled as reported. Nothing is presented as more certain than it is.

## Features

<Features>

- **Showing documents** Pages drawn on a `RawImage` with form field values filled in, rendered off the main thread so the frame keeps its budget.
- **Signature capture** Touch, pen or mouse recorded at event rate with the device's timestamps and pen pressure, drawn as ink.
- **Templates** One record that says where each signature goes, what each signer agrees to, how sure you must be of them and in which order they sign.
- **Several signers** One document, several fields, several people signing in turn on one device, each with their own consent sentence.
- **Audit page and bundle** An appended page that explains the document to whoever reads it, plus the records that back it up.
- **Pseudonymous records** Names and contact details stay out of the records, in one deletable file per signer. Deleting one breaks no hash.

</Features>

## Simple electronic signatures

SignArgs for Unity produces simple electronic signatures: there is no certificate, no cryptographic signature over the PDF and no trusted timestamp. What it gives you is evidence — a signature bound to the pages that were shown, in records anyone can check. Times are device times, and the audit page says so. [Limitations](/projects/signargs/docs/limitations) lists the rest before you build on it.
